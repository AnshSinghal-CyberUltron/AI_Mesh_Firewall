**The three findings that decide the shape of the answer:**

1. **The budget is no longer the constraint on the classifier — the quota is.** B4's levered egress
   architecture drops the marginal cost of a request from W1's $4.87/RPS-month to **$0.45/RPS-month**

### 1.3 Where the 4.50 ms comes from, and why I believe it

The anchor is **`[V]`, not `[D]`**, and it is the single strongest piece of evidence in this whole
workstream:

> NVIDIA published **`microsoft/deberta-v3-xsmall`** — 12 layers, hidden 384, intermediate 1536,
> 6 heads, max relative distance 256, vocab 128 K, **sequence length 512, batch 1** — at
> **1.8 ms on A100-80GB** and **5.6 ms on T4**, TensorRT FP16.
> `[V]` github.com/NVIDIA/TensorRT `release/10.8/demo/DeBERTa/README.md`, fetched 2026-08-29.

That is **our exact model architecture at our exact sequence length**. `Llama-Prompt-Guard-2-22M`'s own
`config.json` is byte-identical to it on every field that matters (`deberta-v2`, 12/384/1536/6,
vocab 128100, `pos_att_type [p2c, c2p]`) `[M]`, and B2 validated the twin at seq512 to **1.0 %**
(104.747 vs 105.785 ms FP32 on CPU) `[M]`.

Bridge to L4, three independent paths, all from NVIDIA's own same-harness demoBERT tables `[V]`:

| Path | Arithmetic | Result |
|---|---|---|
| A — A100 anchor × (L4/A100 p95) | 1.8 × (2.10/1.25) = 1.8 × 1.680 | 3.02 ms |
| A′ — A100 anchor × (L4/A100 avg) | 1.8 × (2.09/1.26) = 1.8 × 1.659 | 2.99 ms |
| B — T4 anchor ÷ (T4/L4 implied) | 5.6 ÷ 1.872 | 2.99 ms |
| B′ — T4 no-plugin | 6.2 ÷ 1.872 | 3.31 ms |

**Method self-validation:** the T4/A100 batch-1 ratio is **3.111** measured on DeBERTa-xsmall@512 and
**3.145** measured on BERT-base@384 — **1.08 % apart** across a different architecture *and* a different
sequence length `[D]`. Batch-1 transformer latency is launch/memory-latency bound, so GPU-to-GPU ratios
are architecture-insensitive. That is what licenses the bridge.

**Batch-2 factor** (2 windows of one request, submitted together): from NVIDIA's L4 BERT-base seq384
FP16 curve, b1 = 2.092 ms and b2 = 3.139 ms `[V]` ⇒ ×1.500. Applied to 3.00 ms ⇒ **4.50 ms** `[D]`.

**Residual risks I did not resolve, stated:** (a) the demoBERT table is labelled "L4" in `release/10.8`
and "A30" in `release/10.13` with byte-identical numbers — B3 resolved this to L4 by three arguments
(TensorRT does not regress 1.9×; A30 has 3.11× L4's bandwidth; MFU clustering puts "disputed = A30" at a
27 % outlier), but NVIDIA never issued an erratum, so it is **inference, not their word**; (b) the anchor
is A100-**80 GB** while the bridge ratio is A100-**40 GB**, which biases the estimate *low*; (c) the
anchor is an average of 100 runs with **no published percentiles anywhere for DeBERTa on any GPU**.

### 1.4 p99 — the honest part

**No p95/p99 exists for DeBERTa on any GPU, from any source `[NF]`.** So I did not guess a tail: I
**simulated the queue**, which is what actually produces the tail at these service times.

Model: Poisson arrivals to one node, one shared queue served by *c* GPU workers with Triton-style dynamic
batching capped at 4 windows, service time taken from NVIDIA's own L4 batch-latency curve scaled by
3.00/2.092 for DeBERTa. 300 k arrivals, 30 k warm-up. Script:
`scratchpad/best/asm_queue2.py`. CPU-side p99 taken as 3.5 ms `[D]` (2.2× the 1.6 ms CPU p50; the measured
p99/p50 ratios in play are 4.7× for `BaseHTTPMiddleware`, 1.13× for the tokenizer, 1.07× for ORT).

**8 L4 total, three layouts, same price for the first and last:**

|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 400 | 8 × `g2-standard-4` (1 L4/node) | 4.50 | 8.85 | 12.15 | 6.10 | 15.65 |
| 400 | **4 × `g2-standard-24` (2 L4/node)** | 4.50 | 5.68 | 9.77 | **6.10** | **13.27** |
| 400 | 2 × `g2-standard-48` (4 L4/node) | 4.50 | 4.50 | 4.92 | 6.10 | 8.42 |
| 800 | 8 × `g2-standard-4` | 4.50 | 12.46 | 17.65 | 6.10 | 21.15 |
| 800 | **4 × `g2-standard-24`** | 4.50 | 10.14 | 11.95 | **6.10** | **15.45** |
| 1,064 | 8 × `g2-standard-4` | 6.02 | 16.81 | 23.05 | 7.62 | 26.55 |

**The finding:** 8 L4 as `4 × g2-standard-24` and 8 L4 as `8 × g2-standard-4` differ by **$803/month**
and by **8 ms of p99 at 1,064 RPS**. `4 × g2-standard-24` and `2 × g2-standard-48` cost **exactly the
same** ($342.095478/L4 — GCP prices the 12-vCPU-per-L4 family identically `[D from V]`); the 4-node
layout trades 3.8 ms of p99 for a third availability zone. **Take the 4-node layout.**

Caveats on the simulation, stated: Poisson arrivals are **conservative** — a least-request LB smooths
per-node arrivals; and the DeBERTa batch-scaling factor is assumed constant across batch, which B3
flagged as unresolved (the per-FLOP penalty is part launch overhead, which amortises, and part memory
indirection, which does not).

### 1.5 Two things that would make it faster, and why I am not banking either

- **Swap to a BERT-family backbone.** `[D]` ~1.0–1.2 ms FP16 on L4 for the same 12L/384 shape (3× faster,
  and INT8 becomes available). On CPU the same swap is **4.53× measured** `[M]` (100.345 → 22.172 ms at
  seq512). **Not banked: there is no injection classifier on an ORT-fusable backbone with a defensible
  published accuracy number** `[M]` — 0 of 10 credible detectors qualify; the one fusable detector with an
  independent score is at 44.58 % average / 34.63 % benign accuracy `[I]`.
- **Speculative dispatch** (send upstream, classify in parallel, abort on a late trip) would hide the
  entire 4.5 ms behind the provider's own 200–800 ms TTFT. **Not banked: E6 rejected it** — it produces a
  false attestation in the customer-facing pipeline trace, uncancellable provider billing, and a
  cross-tenant circuit-breaker DoS at ~10 req/min. Permissible only as an explicitly-consented per-tenant
  opt-in on the Tier-2 verdict alone. **It must not be load-bearing for the SLO.**

### 1.6 What CPU-only would give, for contrast

There is no CPU path to single-digit milliseconds with a transformer that has published accuracy:

| Option | seq512 p50, 8 threads | Tag | Accuracy |
|---|---:|---|---|
| PG2-22M INT8, as shipped | **100.345 ms** | `[M]` | AUC .995 / 88.7 % recall @1 % FPR `[V]` |
| Same, better ONNX export | ~62 ms `[D]` (1.61× `[M]`) | `[D]` | unchanged |
| Identically-shaped BERT, offline-fused + INT8 `QAttention` | **22.172 ms** | `[M]` | **`[NF]` — head does not exist** |
| testsavant-small (BERT 4L/512) | **8.08 ms** | `[M]` | **`[NF]` — publishes nothing** |
| testsavant-tiny (BERT 2L/128) | **1.31 ms** | `[M]` | **`[NF]` — publishes nothing** |

Two windows of the shipping model on CPU is **~200 ms**. The GPU is a **44×** reduction on the stage that
dominates the budget. This is the whole case for lifting the GPU prohibition, and it holds.

### 2.2 Why the quota binds and the budget does not

One L4 running DeBERTa-v3-xsmall@512 through TensorRT FP16 sustains **≈490 windows/s** `[D]`
(saturation knee at window-batch 8, from NVIDIA's own L4 curve — batching past that *hurts*). At 2
windows per request and a 60 % utilisation cap for tail control, that is **133 requests/s per L4**.
Eight L4 — the entire regional quota `[M]` — is **1,064 RPS**.

The money to do that is $2,736.76 (compute) + $540.36 (HA floor) + $460.46 (egress+LB) = **$3,737.58**.
**$1,262.42 of the budget has nothing to buy.** More GPU is not purchasable; more CPU is not needed
(§2.3); more egress buys nothing while the GPU is the wall.

**This corrects B1.** B1 concluded egress caps the 8×L4 design at ~346–511 RPS by linearising W1's
$4.866/RPS-month. That figure predates B4's measured levers. With the trace frame deleted, SSE coalesced,
gzip applied and the provider in-region, the marginal cost is **$0.449/RPS-month** `[D]` — **10.8×
cheaper** — and egress stops being the constraint until ~4,150 RPS.

### 3.2 Where the classifier runs: **in-process on the GPU node. There is no hop.**

`[M]` Live GCP Compute API, this session: `g2-standard-24` returns
`accelerators: [{guestAcceleratorCount: 2, guestAcceleratorType: "nvidia-l4"}], guestCpus: 24` — **the
GPU and the vCPUs are one VM.** Confirmed three ways (machine-types list, machine-types describe,
`docs.cloud.google.com/compute/docs/gpus`). Every G2 shape exists in all three `asia-south1` zones.

This kills the objection that a 12 ms budget cannot absorb a hop to a GPU: **there is no hop to absorb.**
And the alternative is fatal — the *nearest* other region (Delhi) is **≥16.1 ms RTT** by the physics floor
alone `[D]`, Singapore ≥54.7 ms.

**Runtime stack — and this is not a tuning knob:** `[M]` ONNX Runtime 1.29.0's fusion registry
(`MODEL_TYPES`, 19 entries) contains **no `deberta` and no `deberta-v2`**, and fusion is a **graph-level
pass applied before execution-provider assignment**. **ORT CUDA EP would therefore run the same unfused
graph on the GPU.** The viable stacks are (a) native TensorRT, or (b) ORT with the **TensorRT EP**, which
hands the subgraph to TRT and supports the disentangled-attention plugin. The plugin is alive: present on
`main` and `release/11.2`, migrated to `IPluginV3` in 2024.03, touched in the TensorRT 11.0 release
2026-06-02 `[V]` — and at seq512 it is worth **0 %** on A100 and 11 % on T4 anyway, so that risk is
doubly closed.

**Serving:** Triton with the TensorRT backend, one instance group per GPU, **both GPUs on a node behind
one shared queue** (this is the 8 ms of p99 in §1.4), `max_queue_delay_microseconds ≈ 500`,
**`max_batch_size` capped at 4 windows** — the throughput knee is at 8 windows but the tail is materially
better at 4 for a 0.4 % throughput cost `[D]`. Called over the C API / CUDA shared memory on localhost,
never over the network.

### 3.3 The Tier-1 tier — rebuilt, and demoted

| Change | Effect | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Replace **both** inline `ATTACK_PATTERNS` regex loops with one **Hyperscan relaxed-superset gate**, 174/174 patterns compiled | **21.97 ms → 0.160 ms (137×)**, zero measured recall change | `[M]` |
| Port normalise + segmentation to native code (C/Rust-PyO3), byte-identical output | 14.72 ms → **0.434 ms (33.9×)** | `[M]` |
| Gate the segmentation DP behind a **provably-sound dictionary filter** (`DICT \ D_seg`, precomputed offline in 1,052 ms; `|D_seg|` = 2,805 of 272,543 words) | 16.6× less DP work, **0.00 % recall loss by construction** | `[M]` |
| **Demote the injection/jailbreak patterns from `block` to `signal`** | fixes 5/5 + 7/7 + 3/12 measured false positives, **today, with no performance work** | `[M]` |
| `sorted(vocab)` | removes proven per-process **non-determinism** in a security control | `[M]` |

**Why the demotion is not optional.** The shipped scanner **hard-blocks 7/7 realistic benign sentences
containing the word "nominations"** as `jailbreak / tier_0_5 / confidence 0.95`, because
`_deobfuscate_text` mangles it into `"no limitations"` `[M]`. It also hard-blocks 5/5 benign developer
prompts (`` `npm install` ``, `$(date)`) `[M]`. On benign prose the segmentation DP does **18.6 ms of work
and returns zero segmentations from 199 tokens** `[M]`, and it hands an attacker a **93× CPU
amplification** (0.139 ms → 12.91 ms *in native code* at the 10,000-char cap) `[M]`. Published evidence
corroborates the local measurement exactly: a 26-detector production regex pack scores **recall 0.017 on
paraphrase-dominated corpora and 0.978 on template-dominated ones — the same pack** `[I]`. **Meta's
LlamaFirewall ships no regex at all in its injection tier** `[V]`.

**Keep** the value scanners — PII/secret/credential/DoS/transport-anomaly. Those have literal grammar,
regex is the correct and complete tool, and the published FPR for a well-built pack on trigger-word-rich
benign text is **0.9 %** `[I]`. Folded into the Hyperscan gate they cost **0.042 ms**.

### 3.4 Edge and wire format

- **Regional external ALB, Standard Network Tier, HTTP/3 enabled.** Standard Tier requires the regional
  ALB (the global ALB is Premium-only `[V]`); the regional ALB supports HTTP/3 `[V]`. Cost: anycast —
  clients enter Google's network at Mumbai. Free for a Mumbai-centric tenant base.
- **Backend service timeout must be raised explicitly** — the default is 30 s and on an external ALB it
  bounds the *entire* SSE response `[V]`.
- **SSE, `text/event-stream`, adaptive coalescing** ("flush on 16 tokens **or** 120 ms"), **gzip-6 with
  `Z_SYNC_FLUSH` per frame**, `Accept-Encoding`-gated. **No `pipeline_trace` frame — emit a `trace_id`.**
- **Binary framing (WebSocket/gRPC) is not worth it:** at coalesce ×16 the entire JSON
  `chat.completion.chunk` envelope costs **224 compressed bytes for a whole 400-token answer** `[M]`. A
  binary transport would save 13 % of app bytes, 0 % of transport, and every OpenAI-SDK client.
- **nginx:** leave `gzip_types` excluding `text/event-stream` (`deploy/nginx.conf:14`) — nginx buffers its
  own gzip output and would batch the events. The gateway compresses; nginx passes through.
- **No Cloud Armor.** At these rates Standard is **$15,180/mo — 3× the entire budget** `[D]`. Say this out
  loud to whoever signs off.

## 5. WHAT IS NOT PROVEN

Ordered by how much of the recommendation each one carries.

### 5.1 `[D]` — The 4.50 ms GPU forward pass (74 % of the p50 budget). **Cost to convert: $7 and 30 minutes.**

```bash
# g2-standard-8, asia-south1-a  ($0.888583031/hr on-demand [V], inside the existing 8-GPU quota)
docker run --gpus all -it nvcr.io/nvidia/tensorrt:<tag>-py3
git clone -b release/11.2 https://github.com/NVIDIA/TensorRT && cd TensorRT/demo/DeBERTa
python deberta_pytorch2onnx.py --filename ./pg2.onnx --variant <OUR PG2-22M checkpoint>
python deberta_onnx_modify.py ./pg2.onnx --plugin     # -> pg2_plugin.onnx
python deberta_onnx_modify.py ./pg2.onnx              # -> pg2_original.onnx (control)
for M in pg2_plugin pg2_original; do for W in 1 2 4 8 16 32; do
  trtexec --onnx=./$M.onnx --fp16 --optShapes=input_ids:${W}x512,attention_mask:${W}x512 \
          --iterations=1000 --warmUp=200 --percentile=95,99 --noDataTransfers   # NVIDIA-comparable
  trtexec --onnx=./$M.onnx --fp16 --optShapes=input_ids:${W}x512,attention_mask:${W}x512 \
          --iterations=1000 --warmUp=200 --percentile=95,99                     # end-to-end truth
done; done
```

**Five things it must settle, in this order:** (1) is window-batch 1 inside **2.7–4.0 ms**? — if not, the
whole derivation dies and the "L4 vs A30" label call in §1.3 was wrong; (2) **where does throughput
actually saturate** — the 490 vs 1,580 windows/s spread is 3.2× and directly sets $/RPS; (3) **p95/p99
over 1,000+ iterations**, because every published DeBERTa number is an average of 100 runs and *no tail
data exists anywhere* — a 72 W card under sustained load is exactly where tails appear; (4)
`--noDataTransfers` on vs off, to measure the copy cost I estimated at 0.04 ms; (5) **`--int8` with
calibration — does DeBERTa INT8 work on TensorRT at all?** Currently **`[NF]`, a coin-flip**: if it works
throughput roughly doubles; if it fails the way it failed on CPU (missing fusion around the
disentangled-attention gathers) FP16 is the ceiling. **Also worth 20 minutes: `--useCudaGraph`.**

### 5.2 `[NF]` — Real-world detection accuracy. **The most important gap in this document.**

Everything above optimises the *cost* of a verdict. Nothing proves the verdict is *right*.
`[M]` Tier-1 today: 3/10 same-family injections, **0/10 paraphrased**, 5/5 benign developer prompts
hard-blocked, 7/7 "nominations" sentences hard-blocked, 3/12 ordinary tool descriptions hard-blocked.
`[V]` PG2-22M publishes 88.7 % recall @1 % FPR; `[I]` the literature shows an 8–16 point AUC collapse
under leave-one-dataset-out evaluation, ProtectAI at 0 % TPR on harmful requests, Deepset at 82.4 % FPR.
**We have no LODO number for our own deployed configuration.**
**Bake-off:** run this project's own 174-pattern set *and* PG2-22M against deepset/prompt-injections +
NotInject + LLMail-Inject + AgentDojo + AgentHarm, hold out whole datasets, publish the confusion
matrices. Then score the §3.3 demotion (block → signal) before and after. **This gates the demotion and
it gates the product claim.** Nothing here is a substitute for it.

### 5.3 `[D]` — 461 RPS/vCPU / 2.169 ms of CPU per request

Sum of measured components; **never load-tested end to end**. Today's measured value is 0.17–5.4
RPS/vCPU `[M]`. The gap is entirely Tier-1 (63.40 → 0.28 ms) and `redact_all` (32.76 → 0 ms), both backed
by `[M]`, neither shipped. **Bake-off:** do the rewrite, then `oha`/`k6` at a fixed arrival rate against
one host with the classifier off and the prompt length declared. **Until then quote 590–1,064 RPS for
posture A, not 1,064.** (At B4's conservative 10.25 RPS/vCPU the CPU binds first, at ~590 RPS.)

### 5.4 `[NF]` — Does the GFE buffer `text/event-stream`?

No authoritative Google statement in either direction. B4's compression proof covers the **client** stack
(`httpx`) end to end; it does not cover the load balancer. **This is a go/no-go gate on the gzip lever
(1.53×) and possibly on TTFT itself, and it cannot be researched.** **Bake-off:** stand up one regional
external ALB, stream 12 frames at 100 ms with `Content-Encoding: gzip` and `X-Accel-Buffering: no`,
measure client-side arrival gaps. Same harness, real forwarding rule. Also untested: browser
`EventSource` with gzip (Playwright, three browsers).

### 5.5 `[NF]` — Does GCP meter the 74 B/packet of IP+TCP+TLS headers in VM internet egress?

Not stated in any GCP document. I modelled the **conservative** (IP-layer) basis throughout; the L7 basis
makes every RPS number ~13 % better and changes no ranking. **Bake-off:** one VM, 24 h, a known counted
number of application bytes and packets, then read the `Network Internet Egress` SKU off the actual bill.
~$3.

### 5.6 `[NF]` — Is a G2 3-year **resource** CUD actually purchasable?

`COMMITTED_NVIDIA_L4_GPUS = 8` covers the GPU half `[M]`, but `COMMITTED_CPUS = 0` and no
`COMMITTED_G2_CPUS` metric exists. **Worth $479.47/mo per `g2-standard-16`-class node.** If blocked, the
safe fallback is the Flexible (spend-based) CUD at **+26.7 %**, which would raise the BOM from $2,736.76
to ~$3,467 and cut posture-B RPS by ~1,700. **Bake-off:** `gcloud compute commitments create … --plan=36-month`

### 5.7 `[NF]` — Is `NVIDIA_L4_GPUS` raisable above 8 in asia-south1, and in what lead time?

This is the difference between **1,064** and **1,862** RPS at full coverage. **Bake-off:** file the quota
